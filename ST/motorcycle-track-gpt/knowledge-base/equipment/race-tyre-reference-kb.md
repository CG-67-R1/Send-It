# Race Tyre Reference — Knowledge Base
# Road Racing: Compounds, Pressures, Operating Temperatures, Rain Tyres

**Version**: 1.0
**Last Updated**: 2026-08-11
**Scope**: Race slicks, DOT race tyres, and full wet/rain tyres only. Road tyres excluded.
**Update cadence**: Monthly cron job refreshes this file from manufacturer sources.

**CRITICAL RULE**: Always confirm brand, model, compound, and size with the rider before giving pressure advice. Never invent a pressure. If the specific tyre is not in this file, say so and ask for the manufacturer datasheet.

---

## HOW TO READ THIS FILE

- **Cold pressure**: Set with warmers at temperature, or cold if no warmers (take immediately after pit-in if uncertain)
- **Hot pressure**: Measured immediately after pit-in at operating temperature — do not wait for cool-down
- **Hot pressure range**: The target window during a session; adjust cold settings to achieve this range when hot
- **Warmer temp / time**: Manufacturer minimum before going out; not a maximum (exceeding by 10–15 min is usually acceptable, overheating blankets is not)
- **Compound selection**: Track temperature is the primary variable; pace and surface grip are secondary

## RAIN / WET TYRES

### General rain tyre principles

- Rain tyres are cold-pressure tyres — pressure is set cold (no warmers to operating temp, or warmers at 50 °C only)
- Hot pressure is NOT the reference for rain tyres — cold is
- Pressure is adjusted based on the amount of standing water on the track
- Rain tyres operate in a lower temperature window than slicks — too much heat destroys them

### Warmer temperature for rain tyres

- **Standard**: 50 °C for 45 minutes (NOT 80 °C — overheating rain tyres degrades them permanently)
- Some teams run 60–70 °C max; check specific manufacturer recommendation
- If track is drying: reduce warmers or remove — rain tyres should not overheat on a drying track

**Operating temp**: 40–80 °C tyre surface (much lower than slicks)
**Warmers**: 50–60 °C maximum recommended

| Size | Cold bar | Cold psi |
|------|----------|----------|
| Front 120/70 R17 (typical) | ~2.1–2.2 | ~30–32 |
| Rear 190/55 R17 (typical) | ~1.5–1.7 | ~22–25 |

**Note**: Dunlop wet tyre pressures are significantly lower than slick pressures — do not confuse. Verify against the current Dunlop official tyre pressure information sheet.

### Rain tyre safety rules

1. Do NOT run slick pressures on rain tyres. Under-inflated rain tyres overheat on a drying track; over-inflated rain tyres aquaplane.
2. DO check pressures after the first sighting lap (warmers off, wet tyre heats quickly in standing water).
3. On a drying track: reduce pressure in 0.5–1.0 psi steps as the track dries — do not drop below minimum cold setting.
4. NEVER run rain tyres with warmers at slick temperature (80–100 °C). The compound breaks down.
5. When switching from rain to slick (partial dry): rain tyres can be run at reduced temperature until a safe pit window; do not push on rain tyres once the track is predominantly dry.

## OPERATING TEMPERATURE WINDOWS

| Tyre type | Surface temp window (°C) | Notes |
|-----------|--------------------------|-------|
| Full race slick (SC0/SCX, V02/V03 Soft) | 55–90 | Narrow window; cold tear < 55 °C |
| Race slick (SC1/V02 Med, KR A) | 60–100 | Medium window; most ASBK conditions |
| Race slick (SC2/V02 Hard, KR B) | 70–110 | Wider window; hot circuits |
| DOT race (Racetec RR K1/K2, RS12) | 60–120 | Wide window; forgiving |
| Rain tyre (W01, Power Rain) | 30–80 | Drops grip above 80 °C on dry |
| Road-legal sport tyre | 40–120 | Very wide; not track-optimised |

**Tyre surface temperature** is measured at the tyre surface with a probe — not ambient temp. Track temp is a proxy. Surface temp is always 10–25 °C higher than track temp in normal operating conditions.
