# PART 1 — RACE SUSPENSION BRANDS

## HOW TO USE PART 1

Use brand/model data to identify what a rider has fitted and what adjusters are available.
Spring rate selection is rider-weight dependent — see the spring rate calculator section.
Adjuster click ranges are starting points; always note current position before changes.
Never recommend an internal rebuild (oil weight, shim stack, valve) without confirmed make/model/year and a qualified technician.

## ÖHLINS

Origin: Sweden. OEM supplier to MotoGP, WSBK, BSB. Most widely fitted aftermarket brand in ASBK.

### TTX GP Rear Shock (race-level flagship)

Type: Twin-tube, pressurised nitrogen, fully adjustable
Applications: WSBK/BSB/Supersport — available for most 600–1000cc platforms

Key specs (general):
- Piston diameter: 46 mm (GP) / 36 mm (standard TTX)
- Nitrogen pressure: ~10 bar pre-charge
- Adjusters: spring preload, high-speed compression, low-speed compression, rebound, ride height
- Typical adjuster range: compression 0–20 clicks, rebound 0–20 clicks (varies by application)
- Spring free length: ~150 mm (common)
- Spring rate range: ~55–120 N/mm (application-dependent; 80–95 N/mm typical 600cc Supersport)
- Ride height range: typically ±10 mm from baseline

Starting point (600cc Supersport, ~75 kg rider in gear):
- Sag: 28–35 mm race; 30–40 mm track day
- Free sag: 8–18 mm (per Öhlins manual)
- Spring rate: 80–95 N/mm common; 95–110 N/mm for heavier riders or 1000cc
- Compression: mid-range baseline; increase 2–3 clicks for bumpy/high-speed tracks
- Rebound: mid-range baseline; slower (fewer clicks out) for smooth tracks, faster for bumpy

### NIX30 Fork Cartridge (road-racing standard)

Type: 30 mm closed cartridge kit — replaces OEM internals
Applications: Supersport 600, Superbike 1000 (fitment-specific)

Key specs:
- Cartridge diameter: 30 mm
- Oil weight: 7.5W SAE baseline; 5W for more compliant, heavier for stiffer
- Oil level: ~160 mm from top of tube (with cartridge installed); check application manual
- Combined spring rate: ~9.75 N/mm when two springs used in series (application-specific)
- Adjusters: compression (external, typically 20–25 clicks), rebound (external, typically 20–25 clicks)
- Spring rates: typically 0.85–1.05 N/mm for 600cc; 0.95–1.20 N/mm for 1000cc

Starting point:
- Sag: 30–38 mm race; 35–42 mm track day
- Oil level changes: +10 mm = stiffer mid-to-end stroke; -10 mm = softer, more compliant
- Start compression mid-range; adjust 1–2 clicks at a time

### FGRT300 / FG900 Fork (full race replacement)

Type: Complete race fork leg — not a cartridge upgrade
Applications: BSB/WSBK OEM supply; aftermarket for specific platforms
Key specs: 43 mm (standard) or 48 mm (large bore); TTX cartridge-style internals; fully adjustable spring preload, compression (hi/lo), rebound

## SPRING RATE SELECTION GUIDE

### Fork spring rate (N/mm = kg/mm — same number, different unit)

| Rider weight in gear | 600cc Supersport | 1000cc Superbike |
|----------------------|------------------|------------------|
| < 65 kg              | 0.80–0.88        | 0.88–0.95        |
| 65–75 kg             | 0.85–0.95        | 0.95–1.05        |
| 75–85 kg             | 0.90–1.00        | 1.00–1.10        |
| 85–95 kg             | 0.95–1.05        | 1.05–1.15        |
| > 95 kg              | 1.00–1.10        | 1.10–1.20        |

### Rear spring rate (N/mm)

| Rider weight in gear | 600cc Supersport | 1000cc Superbike |
|----------------------|------------------|------------------|
| < 65 kg              | 70–80            | 80–90            |
| 65–75 kg             | 80–90            | 90–100           |
| 75–85 kg             | 88–98            | 95–110           |
| 85–95 kg             | 95–105           | 105–118          |
| > 95 kg              | 100–115          | 110–125          |

These are indicative ranges. Track surface (smooth vs bumpy), riding position, and chassis geometry all influence the correct rate. Start at mid-range; adjust based on sag measurement.

### Sag reference

| Measurement             | Race target | Track-day target |
|-------------------------|-------------|------------------|
| Fork sag (rider on, full gear)  | 30–38 mm | 35–42 mm |
| Rear sag (rider on, full gear)  | 28–35 mm | 30–40 mm |
| Free sag (rear, rider off)      | 5–15 mm  | 5–15 mm  |

Free sag < 5 mm = spring too stiff or preload too high.
Free sag > 20 mm = spring too soft.

### Fork oil weight guide

| Weight | Viscosity effect | Typical use |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|
| 2.5W/5W | Lighter/softer | Cold climates, bumpy tracks, compliance priority |
| 7.5W | Standard race baseline | General track use (most Öhlins, Andreani specs) |
| 10W | Heavier/stiffer | Smooth tracks, high-speed circuits |
| 15W | Very stiff | Rarely used; specific high-load applications only |

### Adjuster direction reference

| Adjuster | Clockwise (IN) | Counter-clockwise (OUT) |
|----------|---------------|------------------------|
| Compression | Stiffer | Softer |
| Rebound | Slower | Faster |
| Preload | More preload | Less preload |

Always count from fully closed (clockwise to stop) outward. Never force past stops.

### Common suspension diagnostics

| Symptom | Check first | Then |
|---------|-------------|------|
| Front dives on brakes | Fork compression too soft / springs too soft | Check sag; increase compression 2 clicks |
| Rear bouncing on exits | Rebound too fast | Slow rebound 2 clicks (CW) |
| Chattering on exits | Tyre pressure/temp first | Check hot pressure; then slow rebound |
| Bike won't turn | Check sag, tyre pressure, entry speed | Geometry last resort |
| High-side on throttle | Rear rebound too slow | Faster rebound 1–2 clicks (CCW) |
| Front push mid-corner | Tyre pressure first | Increase fork compression slightly |

# PART 2 — RACE TYRE REFERENCE

## HOW TO USE PART 2

Cold pressure: set with warmers at temperature, or cold if no warmers.
Hot pressure: measured immediately after pit-in at operating temperature. Do not wait for cool-down.
Hot pressure range: target window during a session. Adjust cold settings to achieve this range when hot.
Warmer temp/time: manufacturer minimum before going out.
Compound selection: track temperature is the primary variable; pace and surface grip are secondary.

## RAIN / WET TYRES

### General principles

Rain tyres are cold-pressure tyres — pressure is set cold (warmers at 50 °C only, not 80 °C).
Hot pressure is not the reference for rain tyres — cold is.
Pressure is adjusted based on the amount of standing water on the track.
Too much heat destroys rain tyres; never run at slick warmer temperatures.

### Rain tyre warmer temperature

Standard: 50 °C for 45 minutes. Some teams up to 60–70 °C max.
Do NOT use 80–100 °C slick temperatures on rain tyres — the compound breaks down permanently.

### Michelin Power Rain pressure table

| Condition    | Cold front psi | Cold rear psi |
|--------------|---------------|--------------|
| Soaking wet  | 34.8          | 34.8         |
| Wet          | 34.8          | 31.9         |
| Drying       | 33.0          | 26.0         |

### Bridgestone W01 Rain

Warmers: 50 °C / 45 minutes.

| Size              | Position | Cold psi    |
|-------------------|----------|-------------|
| W01 F 120/600 R17 | Front    | 32          |
| W01 F 110/590 R17 | Front    | 28          |
| W01 R 190/650 R17 | Rear     | 29–33.5 base|
| W01 R 165/630 R17 | Rear     | 29–33.5     |
| W01 R 140/620 R17 | Rear     | 29          |

Water level adjustments (Bridgestone official):
- Very wet (> 1/16 inch standing water): + 1.5 psi
- Wet track: + 0 psi (baseline)
- Drying track (limited wet areas): - 1.5 psi

### Rain tyre safety rules

1. Do NOT run slick pressures on rain tyres.
2. Check pressures after the first sighting lap — rain tyres heat quickly in standing water.
3. On a drying track: reduce pressure in 0.5–1.0 psi steps. Do not drop below minimum cold setting.
4. Never run rain tyres with warmers at slick temperature (80–100 °C).
5. When track is predominantly dry: pit for slicks — do not push rain tyres on a dry surface.

## Photo diagnosis protocol

### A. Intake — ask only what the image does not show (batch ≤ 4 questions)

1. Front or rear? (must know)
2. Brand / model / compound (if advising pressure or compound change)
3. Hot pit-in pressure (measured immediately after session)
4. Track temp / session length / warmers used (if thermal cause is ambiguous)

If the photo is unclear, ask for a second shot — do not guess the pattern at High confidence.

### B. Observe in this order (spatial before texture)

Ignore colour overlays, arrows, or captions drawn on the photo unless the user confirms accuracy.

1. Orientation — which side is centreline vs shoulder vs extreme edge? Use rim/brake/hub if visible.
2. Zone — where is the damage relative to lean-angle zones?
3. Band geometry — width (narrow finger-width vs broad outer-third), continuity around circumference (full ring vs intermittent patches), width uniformity as you follow the band.
4. Surface morphology (only after zone and band) — undercut/peelable flaps vs shallow smear, arc toward centre vs parallel ridges, groove-edge lips, sheen/blue-green bloom (oils, not tearing), rubber ball size and stringing.
5. Classify → cite this file → apply fix order from companion guide.

### C. Anti-confusion rule

Do not map "rough / shredded / rippled rubber" to cold tear by default. Band location + width + continuity + depth decide the class. Texture is a tie-breaker.

Cold vs hot — band-width discriminator:

| Cue              | Cold tear           | Hot tear                  |
|------------------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| Affected band    | Often narrower      | Often wider / more diffuse|
| Tear depth       | Deep; nail lifts flaps | Shallow; nail won't undercut |
| Look             | Rip into good rubber | Melted / balled-up surface|
| Contact-patch    | Small (often high pressure) | Large flexing (often low pressure) |

A tear band can be obvious even when surrounding surface looks smooth. Smooth ≠ healthy pressure. Class by band + depth + ball size, not overall look.

### D. Confidence

| Evidence | Confidence |
|----------|------------|
| Clear zone + band geometry + morphology; front/rear known | High–Medium |
| One tight close-up; zone or circumference unknown | Medium–Low — ask for wider shot |
| Ambiguous depth (cold vs hot) | Ask fingernail test; Medium until answered |
| Blue sheen only, no tear structure | High — discoloration, not a tear class |

## Lean-angle zone map

| Zone | Where on the tyre | Typical load |
|------|--------------------|--------------|
| Z0   | Centre crown       | Straight, drive, braking upright |
| Z1   | Inner shoulder     | Light lean / transition |
| Z2   | Mid-shoulder       | Typical track lean |
| Z3   | Outer third / extreme edge | Max lean |

State findings as: Front|Rear · Z0–Z3 · continuous|intermittent · narrow|wide · deep-undercut|shallow-smear|raised-groove-lip|blue-bloom|ball-size-mm

## Rubber balls / slag / edge aggregation

On slicks and soft compounds, look at the extreme edge (Z3):

| Ball size and look | Interpretation |
|--------------------|----------------|
| Small crumbs ~2–3 mm, modest edge line | Often normal worked tyre / acceptable heat |
| Larger balls ~3–5 mm, rubber stringing, heavy edge heap | Too hot — raise hot pressure in small steps and/or check compound |
| Foreign track marbles stuck randomly | Pickup — clean; not a tear class |

## Pattern visual signatures

### 1. Cold tear

Must-see:
- Damage on drive/loaded shoulder (often Z1–Z2 on rear).
- Band often narrower than hot-tear damage for the same tyre.
- Tears deep and undercut — raised flaps or "fingernail / half-moon" lips.
- Adjacent rubber often still relatively intact; sharp contrast between torn band and smoother zones.

Texture: chunky, jagged, peelable ridges; sharp crusty edges — not melted-rounded.

Lookalikes: hot tear (wider, shallower, smeared/balled, arcs inward); suspension tear (width varies or skips); geometry tear on front.

Confirm: "Can you get a fingernail under a flap and lift rubber?" → Yes supports cold tear. Check hot pressure vs brand window — often high / small contact patch.

---

### 2. Hot tear / overheating tear

Must-see:
- Affected area wider / more diffuse than classic cold tear; looks worked and soft.
- Tears shallower; hard to undercut with a nail.
- Often arcs or trails running toward Z0 centre (centrifugal throw of softened rubber).
- Rear may show rough band on both lean sides when pressure is too low.
- May tear while face still looks smooth — do not require melted texture to call hot tear.
- Edge rubber balls oversized (~3–5 mm) and/or stringing strongly support overheating.

Texture: smeared, melted, "galled," thinly stripped, or melted balled-up crumbs — not thick peelable chunks. After pressure corrected, earlier hot tear may leave dark residue while grain heals toward fine even wear — residue ≠ active new tear.

Lookalikes: cold tear (deep flaps, narrower), soft-compound overload/blister precursor, rain tyre cooked on dry line.

Confirm: nail under flaps? Usually no. Hot pressure often low or compound too soft for pace/temp.

---

### 3. Suspension-related wear

Must-see:
- Tear width not uniform as you follow the circumference — thick then thin then thick.
- Or damage intermittent: torn sector → clean sector → torn again (not a continuous ring).
- May coexist with pressure-like texture; spatial irregularity is the diagnostic tell.

Confirm: second photo around the tyre. Spring rate suitability for rider weight; rebound/compression history.

Subtype — raised groove edge:
- Macro: rubber lip raised on one side of a groove, other side flatter.
- Leading-edge raised lip (edge that meets road first in rotation) → rebound often too slow.
- Trailing-edge raised lip → rebound often too fast.
- Only diagnose from a sharp groove macro; ask for one if overview is soft.

---

### 4. Geometry — not enough weight on front

Must-see:
- Almost always front tyre.
- Narrow tear band ~5–10 mm wide (finger-width), typically Z1–Z2, not the whole outer third.
- Band usually uniform and continuous around the circumference.
- Extreme edge (Z3) may look under-used relative to the torn mid-band.

Lookalikes: hot tear on rear (wider, arc to centre), too-much-front geometry (outer third, wide).

Confirm: front vs rear; approximate band width vs tyre half-width. Geometry only after pressure + compound confirmed in window.

Authority note: keep light front = narrow mid band; heavy front = outer third. Do not invent from conflicting web sources.

---

### 5. Geometry — too much weight on front

Must-see:
- Almost always front tyre.
- Damage dominates outer third (Z3) — large affected area.
- Inner boundary of bad wear often follows a clean circumferential line.

Texture: heavy scrub / tear on outer third; centre may look healthier.

Confirm: mostly outer third or a thin mid strip? Soft front end + forward weight bias history. Hot pressures in window first.

---

### 6. Graining

Must-see: Surface covered in rolled pills / small rubber balls or "rolled dough" skin — rubber moved, not deep-ripped flaps. Often early session / cool surface / sliding before full heat.

---

### 7. Blistering

Must-see: Raised bubbles, blisters, or local skin separation; may burst into craters. Localised heat pockets, not a uniform circumferential band.

---

### 8. Cupping / scalloping (often front)

Must-see: Alternating high/low cups around the circumference — wavy height, lump-like, not just surface grain. Best confirmed by hand: run palm around tyre — feel repeating dips/humps. Rider may report vibration or roar at speed.

Causes bias: worn/poor damping, imbalance, pressure extremes — mechanical inspection before storytelling.

---

### 9. Feathering / sawtooth (treaded tyres)

Must-see: Groove or block edges smooth one direction, sharp/stepped the other (fingernail test along the block). Common on treaded fronts from brake + turn-in scrub; rear from drive torque or chain alignment.

---

### 10. Chunking

Must-see: Missing rubber with sharp voids / craters; local, abrupt loss of material. Heat + abrasion / bond failure — compound and pressure first.

---

### 11. Pickup / marbling

Must-see: Discrete blobs or crumbs stuck onto the surface (track rubber), not continuous tear structure of the carcass. Pickup sits proud; underlying grain may still be fine.

---

### 12. Flat-spot / squared centre (Z0)

Must-see: Centre strip more worn or squared-off vs shoulders. Often lighter grey / dusty centre band vs darker unused shoulders; tread grooves may vanish at Z0 while shoulders still show depth. Straight miles, high hot pressure, or TC-induced sliding.

Fix bias: pressure toward window; compound; gearing/throttle. Not geometry first.

---

### 13. Rain tyre overheating on drying line

Must-see: Rain/intermediate carcass; glossy, greasy, polished smear, often asymmetric where the dry line loaded the tyre. Rapid onset story from rider.

Action bias: tyre choice / avoid the dry line — not suspension first.

---

### 14. Blue / green / purple discoloration

Must-see: Iridescent blue-green-purple bloom or oily sheen after cooling — oils migrated to surface. Not a tear class. Signal of heat cycles used; performance may fade over many cycles — do not declare tyre dead from colour alone.

Lookalikes: wet glare / flash reflection — ask if colour remains in shade after cool-down.

---

### 15. Good / healthy wear ("beach tide")

Must-see: Even, fine directional grain across the used lean zones. No deep undercut flaps, no melted smear arcs, no intermittent shredded sectors. Edge use matches rider lean.

Note: racers may trade tyre life for pace — "good longevity wear" does not mean maximum grip setup.

## Decision tree (photo → class)

1. Blue/iridescent bloom only, no tear structure? → Discoloration (heat-cycle oils). Not a tear fix.
2. Fine even grain, no deep flaps / smear arcs / intermittent shred? → Good wear. Hold; log pressures.
3. Front + narrow (~5–10 mm) continuous mid-shoulder band (Z1–Z2)? → Geometry: not enough front weight (after pressure window check).
4. Front + wide outer-third (Z3) wear with clean circumferential inner edge? → Geometry: too much front weight (after pressure/compound).
5. Tear width varies OR torn/clean/torn around circumference? → Suspension-related. Groove lip raised one side → rebound hint.
6. Deep undercut / nail-liftable flaps, narrower band, sharp contrast? → Cold tear → pressure/temp window first.
7. Wider/shallow smear, arcs toward centre, oversized edge balls (~3–5 mm), or tear band despite smooth face? → Hot tear / overheat → pressure + compound + pace.
8. Pills / bubbles / cups / sawtooth / chunks / stuck marbles? → Graining / blistering / cupping / feathering / chunking / pickup.
9. Still unclear → ask Front/Rear, second circumference shot, nail test, hot pressure, ball size estimate. Confidence: Low–Medium. No numeric setup leap.

## Context confounders (do not mis-class)

- Circuit direction asymmetry: clockwise tracks wear one shoulder harder — not automatically suspension/geometry.
- Abrasive / new track mix: surface can look harsh without classic tear morphology — ask venue.
- Healed hot tear residue: dark stain after pressure fix with improving grain — history, not active tear.
- Conflicting geometry blogs: keep light-front = narrow mid band; heavy-front = outer third.

## What never to do

- Invent tear depth, circumference continuity, or front/rear if not stated/visible.
- Jump to suspension/geometry before pressure + thermal/compound context (fix priority).
- Treat blue oils as proof the tyre is finished.
- Equate any roughness with cold tear.
- Invent a pressure number for an unlisted tyre.
