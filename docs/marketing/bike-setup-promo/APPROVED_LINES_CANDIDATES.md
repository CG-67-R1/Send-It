# Bike Setup — approved lines and candidates

Product name is always **RoadRacer**. Never “Send-It” as a brand. Australian English: tyre, kerb, track day, paddock, analyse.

## How the stars work

Same gate as `DigiMark_Response/APPROVED_LINES.md`:

- No stars = already approved. Speak it word for word.
- `*Wrapped in asterisks*` = candidate. Do not put it in a Reel, caption, voiceover, or store until the owner strips the stars.

## Already approved (carry forward unchanged)

From the DigiMark print sheet.

> RoadRacer is a pocket coach for track-day riders and club racers. You tell it how you ride, then it helps you learn the circuit, set the bike, and get a briefing that matches your level — from first track days through racing.

> It is built around Australian road-race circuits, with Coach, Bike Setup, Track Details, calendar, and Q&A in one place.

> Coach matches how you ride.

> RoadRacer AI uses your answers so Coach and Bike Setup stay at the right level of detail. You can change how you ride anytime in Settings.

> I built it because track-day and club racers need coaching and setup help on the phone.

> Most of us get to the paddock with a phone full of screenshots and no one to ask. Club racing and track days don’t come with a crew chief.

> Time to send it.

**Onboarding closer only.** “Time to send it” is not a tagline and not a setup-post sign-off.

### Legal super (approved — use in full)

> Coach, Bike Setup, Bike Balance, Q&A, and rules answers are informational aids only. They are not a substitute for a qualified technician, coach, or official rule book.

### Privacy line (approved)

> Your profile, avatar, setups, and track notes stay on your device. AI chats you send go to the RoadRacer API and may be processed by OpenAI; chat history is not kept on our server after the reply.

Do not shorten the legal super to fit a frame. Hold the frame longer or split it across two cards.

## New candidates (unapproved until stars are stripped)

Six sentences, each checkable against a screen that ships today. No pressures, click counts, sag figures, sprocket pairs, lap times, or user counts.

### 1 — Day sheet

*Write the day sheet in the paddock: pressures, sag, clicks, and notes stay on the phone, session by session.*

Screen: Bike Setup Sheet. Names fields the sheet holds; states no values.

### 2 — Tyre from a photo

*Photograph the tyre, add the facts a picture cannot show, then analyse the wear with Bike Setup AI.*

Screen: Tyre Wear Analysis. Matches the in-app “facts a picture cannot show” framing.

### 3 — Gearing

*Gearing Guide works through front and rear sprockets and where the powerband sits, then hands the brief to Bike Setup AI.*

Screen: Gearing Guide. Names no tooth counts, speeds, or lap times.

### 4 — Bike Setup AI

*Bike Setup AI takes suspension, gearing, and tyre questions in a setup chat of its own, pitched at how you ride.*

Screen: Bike Setup AI. Does not call it a race engineer and does not promise a correct answer.

### 5 — Skill level

*Tell RoadRacer how you ride and Bike Setup keeps the detail at your level — change it anytime in Settings.*

Restates the approved level line for a setup-led post.

### 6 — Bike Setup Basics

*Bike Setup Basics is an interactive suspension diagram — tap any of the twelve hotspots for road versus track guidance.*

Twelve is countable in `app/src/data/bikeSetupBasics.ts` (seven front, five rear).

## Never say

- Any product name other than RoadRacer
- Best / #1 / unbeatable
- Race engineer as a description of the app (the crew-chief line above is the rider’s problem, not RoadRacer’s job title)
- A tyre pressure, click count, sag figure, sprocket pair, or ride-height number as advice
- Lap times, personal bests, or modelled times
- User counts, ratings, testimonials
- “Upload your data”
- News as a reason to download
- Bike Balance as the opening hook
- A GitHub URL or the Vercel preview slug as a public CTA
